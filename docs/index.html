Built around how Alaska eats

Star ratings won't tell you which roadhouse is open this season, or where to find a reindeer dog. Alaska Eats is organized the way people here eat: halibut, salmon and crab, Anchorage's global kitchens, roadhouses and lodges, reindeer dogs and sourdough, each hand-checked place backed by a source from fall 2026. In Anchorage, places the Health Department scored show their latest inspection, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos everywhere.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out between towns.

What stays on your phone

Alaska Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Alaska Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The seafood, global kitchens, roadhouse, reindeer dog and sourdough lists are our own research from fall 2026: a dated 2025 or 2026 page names each place (the place's own site, local news or a tourism listing), or an older one does and the place holds an active Anchorage food permit. The wider directory of 1,905 restaurants comes from Overture Maps' open place data, checked in Anchorage against the Health Department's food permits and the Clerk's liquor licenses, keeping the kinds of listing that proved reliable there.

How do I check an Anchorage restaurant's inspection?

The Anchorage Health Department scores its regular inspections, reinspections and enforcement visits out of 100 and publishes the scores. Alaska Eats shows the latest score for 458 places, with its date and the department's next step, exactly as published, and links each one to the department's own reports. The department's open-data file hasn't been updated since May 19, 2026, so a newer visit may be on its site and not here yet.

What about restaurants outside Anchorage?

The Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation inspects them, but it publishes no results in bulk and its website doesn't allow automated reading, so the app shows no inspections outside Anchorage. The places themselves come from open map data and our own checks.

Are summer-only places listed?

Yes. Many lodges, roadhouses and seafood shacks open only in summer, and a place closed for the winter is never marked closed for good. Where our research found a place's season, its hand-checked note says so; check the hours before a long drive.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
